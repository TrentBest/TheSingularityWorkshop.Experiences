What this picture makes explicit

Story time ≠ reading order. A scene in chapter one may depict Year 20, while chapter two reveals Year 3. The timeline preserves the world's chronology; the book preserves the reader's sequence.

Parallel time is first-class. Character arcs, institutional calendars, planets, flashbacks, and competing accounts do not have to be flattened onto one universal clock.

Published editions are snapshots. Revising canon can change a new edition's coverage without silently mutating a published one. This prototype's sample data is illustrative and is not yet wired to the StoryForge C# domain library.

Intended integration: StoryForge domain queries → selected timeline/coverage model → Visualize frame → GUI.Core neutral scene → GUI.SVG output; Renderer can choose representation detail for the observer. No dependency on Unity or a specific authoring host is required by the story data.
