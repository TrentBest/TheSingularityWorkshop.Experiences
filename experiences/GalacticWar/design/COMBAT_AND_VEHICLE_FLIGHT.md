# Combat, exoskeletons, and spacecraft

## Combat feel

The target is immersive, first-person, high-intensity combat with the immediacy and kinetic spectacle associated with raWWar. The player should feel present inside the action rather than issuing detached commands to an avatar.

Combat should reward awareness, positioning, timing, movement, target prioritization, and coordination—not only aim speed or damage output.

## Exoskeletons and powered armor

Exoskeletons are a central visual and gameplay bridge between human-scale action and larger machines. They can extend strength, endurance, protection, mobility, sensor reach, and tool use.

They should not all be interchangeable suits with different statistics. Their form and capability should express their intended role: reconnaissance, rescue, engineering, heavy assault, support, or other roles still to be defined.

The suit should make the player feel more capable while retaining meaningful limits: power, heat, load, stability, damage, maintenance, and environmental constraints.

## Spacecraft dogfighting

The signature flight fantasy is momentum-aware, three-dimensional combat.

Example maneuver:
1. Acquire and maintain a target lock.
2. Rotate the spacecraft through a 180-degree flip.
3. Continue to engage the target while the craft's existing momentum carries it along its trajectory.
4. Use independently controlled axis thrusters to correct translation and orientation.
5. Reassess the engagement geometry and choose the next maneuver.

The point is to separate facing from travel direction. A craft can point one way while coasting another, allowing dramatic reversals, strafing, drift, and controlled corrections.

Target lock should assist awareness and tracking without becoming an automatic victory button. Countermeasures, line of sight, sensor conditions, range, occlusion, electronic warfare, and target maneuvering are candidate factors.

## Fictional gravity-field technology

The setting assumes gravity-field generator technology. A pilot can survive aggressive acceleration and rotation without being crushed by their own body. This keeps the experience fun and readable rather than forcing realistic human acceleration limits.

The fiction should still define what the field does and does not protect: pilot and crew inertial compensation; loose objects and internal cargo; interaction with external gravity; field power and failure modes; overlapping or disrupted fields; and effects on nearby objects and other ships.

These are open worldbuilding decisions. The field is not a substitute for propulsion or momentum management.

## Flight model principles

- Distinguish orientation, angular velocity, linear velocity, and acceleration.
- Preserve momentum unless a defined force or control system changes it.
- Make translation and rotation independently understandable to the player.
- Give axis thrusters a legible purpose and controllable response.
- Provide readable instruments and visual cues for velocity, target geometry, and relative motion.
- Allow accessibility options and input assistance without removing the underlying flight fantasy.
- Keep fictional assist systems explicit; do not silently violate the rules established by the universe.

## Scaling ship sizes

Ship scale should change the experience qualitatively:
- small craft emphasize pilot skill, awareness, and rapid maneuvering;
- larger craft introduce inertia, crew, systems, damage control, and operational tradeoffs;
- capital ships make positioning, coordination, logistics, and mission objectives central;
- fleets create formation, command, reconnaissance, supply, and alliance problems.

The player should not simply pilot a larger version of the same fighter. At larger scales, the meaningful decisions and available forms of agency change.

## Questions for later design

- What is the first controllable vehicle?
- Is flight Newtonian-inspired, heavily assisted, or a hybrid?
- How are target lock and off-boresight weapons represented?
- What are the meaningful costs and counters to gravity-field generators?
- Which ship systems can players operate directly, and which are crew/automation responsibilities?
- How does multi-crew play work across different manifestations?
