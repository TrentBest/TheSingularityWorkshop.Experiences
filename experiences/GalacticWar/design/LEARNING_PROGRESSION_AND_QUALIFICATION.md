# Learning Progression and Professional Qualification

**Status:** candidate design contract; not approved world canon.

This document turns the learning principles into a progression model that can support schools, apprenticeships, professional work, and later high-responsibility roles. It is deliberately not a military career ladder or a universal character-level system.

## The governing idea

**Education expands choice. Qualification establishes bounded authority. Experience builds judgment.**

These are related, but they are not interchangeable:

- Education helps a person understand a subject, discover interests, and connect ideas.
- Training develops a particular capability through guided practice and feedback.
- Qualification provides evidence that a person can perform a defined task to a defined standard under stated conditions.
- Experience develops judgment in changing real situations; it may deepen trust and responsibility but does not erase formal safety requirements.

A person can be highly educated without wanting a hazardous profession. A qualified operator can still be inexperienced. A veteran can know a great deal that is not captured by a credential. The world should represent these distinctions.

## A progression that is not a funnel

A learner's path is better represented as a branching portfolio than as a single level:

1. **Encounter:** see a subject in ordinary life; ask questions; observe people doing the work.
2. **Explore:** try safe, low-consequence activities; compare interests; learn foundational ideas.
3. **Study:** follow a subject with instruction, reading, experiments, projects, and collaboration.
4. **Practice:** rehearse a specific capability with feedback, including realistic but bounded simulations.
5. **Demonstrate:** show the capability against explicit criteria, in a context appropriate to its risk.
6. **Qualify:** receive a scoped authorization where the role requires one.
7. **Apply:** use the capability in supervised or appropriately independent work.
8. **Develop:** maintain currency, learn advanced techniques, mentor others, or move into a different specialty.

These are not mandatory global stages. Someone may enter through an apprenticeship, transfer knowledge from another field, learn independently, or return to study later in life. Different disciplines use different sequences and standards.

## Foundational learning and discovery

Schools and public learning spaces should expose learners to a broad, connected world rather than asking them to select a permanent profession too early.

Useful foundations include:

- mathematics, measurement, logic, and systems thinking;
- physical sciences, materials, energy, and motion;
- biology, health, ecology, and closed-loop habitats;
- computing, robotics, communications, and information literacy;
- navigation, geography, astronomy, and spatial reasoning;
- history, languages, culture, ethics, and civic institutions;
- art, design, storytelling, and creative problem-solving;
- collaboration, communication, conflict resolution, and practical care.

These subjects should appear in the environment and in projects. Learners might map a station's transit network, build a harmless sensor, compare competing historical records, grow food in a habitat model, or help explain a public science exhibit. Activities should reward curiosity and good reasoning, not merely speed or rote completion.

## Disciplines and pathways

Pathways should overlap and allow movement between them. Examples include:

| Pathway | Example capabilities | Possible settings |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering and fabrication | diagnose systems, design components, repair, test materials | workshops, shipyards, infrastructure teams |
| Science and research | form hypotheses, measure, analyze evidence, reproduce results | laboratories, observatories, field expeditions |
| Medicine and care | assess, communicate, protect privacy, respond within scope | clinics, hospitals, rescue teams |
| Navigation and transport | plan routes, interpret traffic, operate craft, manage cargo | ports, ships, route-control centers |
| Civic and diplomatic work | mediate, interpret institutions, negotiate, coordinate public services | councils, embassies, community organizations |
| Education and culture | teach, curate knowledge, preserve history, create experiences | schools, libraries, museums, cultural venues |
| Exploration and field work | prepare expeditions, observe safely, document, adapt | frontier settlements, research vessels, remote sites |
| Security and emergency response | assess risk, coordinate, protect people, de-escalate | stations, rescue services, protective organizations |
| Military service | operate within a command structure, coordinate specialist capabilities, follow lawful orders | military institutions and deployments |

This list is illustrative, not exhaustive or ranked. Military service is one vocation among many. No education institution should quietly turn every learner toward combat.

## Qualification is scoped, evidenced, and contextual

A qualification should answer four questions:

- **What task or responsibility is authorized?**
- **Under what conditions, equipment, and operating limits?**
- **What evidence demonstrated competence?**
- **Who recognizes, maintains, suspends, or revokes the authorization?**

A credential is not a blanket permission. A person qualified to pilot a small station shuttle is not thereby qualified to command a capital ship. A simulator result may establish readiness for a supervised practical assessment, but cannot silently grant authority over real passengers, hazardous machinery, medical decisions, or weapons.

Qualifications may be portable between institutions when their standards and evidence are recognized. Differences in equipment, law, environment, or local procedure may require bridging instruction or reassessment. Credentials should have provenance and a clear issuer; forged, expired, suspended, or context-incompatible credentials must not be treated as valid.

## Safety and increasing responsibility

Training environments should make the boundary between rehearsal and consequential operation unmistakable.

A useful risk progression is:

- **Open exploration:** safe demonstrations and reversible experiments.
- **Guided practice:** supervised tasks with constrained systems and recoverable failure.
- **Simulation:** realistic scenarios whose outcomes do not endanger real people or infrastructure.
- **Supervised operation:** real equipment or duties with a qualified supervisor and explicit limits.
- **Independent responsibility:** only after the applicable qualification, authorization, and local requirements are met.

The exact gates vary by discipline. Medical care, piloting, power systems, heavy fabrication, security, and military operations should not share one generic pass/fail test. The system should preserve the reason a gate exists, not just its completion flag.

For younger learners, activities should be age-appropriate and focused on discovery, creativity, collaboration, and safe practice. The Experience must not infer that a child visitor is an in-world combatant, nor make dangerous service the reward for finishing school. Audience-specific content and safeguarding rules remain a prerequisite to implementation.

## How capability should be represented

Avoid collapsing all learning into one level, XP bar, or universal skill score. A useful capability record can distinguish:

- subject knowledge and its evidence;
- practical skills and demonstrated proficiency;
- qualifications and their scope;
- supervised experience and completed work;
- recency or currency where skills can decay;
- mentors, institutions, and references;
- learner goals and interests, which may change.

The representation should support uncertainty. Completing a lesson is evidence of participation; it is not necessarily evidence of mastery. A successful assessment is evidence against a particular standard; it is not proof of infallibility. Reputation and personal trust may inform opportunities but must not replace required qualifications.

## Institutions as part of the living galaxy

Schools, academies, guilds, clinics, research institutes, and apprenticeship networks should have recognizable identities and constraints. They may differ in teaching methods, equipment, standards, access, funding, prestige, and relationships with local communities.

Institutions can cooperate or disagree. A frontier workshop may teach practical repair exceptionally well but lack a capital ship's specialized equipment. A prestigious academy may have excellent laboratories and political blind spots. A community clinic may recognize a practitioner's experience while requiring additional local training for unfamiliar technology.

These differences create meaningful choices without making one institution the universal best answer. They also give the station and its people continuity: instructors have careers, apprentices graduate, laboratories need supplies, alumni return, and local events affect what can be taught.

## A candidate first learning journey at Vesper Reach

The first station slice can demonstrate the model without building a full education simulator:

1. A visitor encounters several public demonstrations in the Learning Commons and sees different professions at work across the station.
2. They choose one small, safe project—for example, investigating a sensor discrepancy in a classroom model.
3. An instructor introduces the relevant concepts and lets the learner test competing explanations.
4. A workshop or simulator provides guided practice with visible feedback.
5. The learner presents findings and receives a record of what they actually demonstrated.
6. The instructor suggests optional next steps: another subject, a mentor conversation, an apprenticeship observation, or simply exploring the station.
7. Later, if the visitor chooses a profession, a separate role-specific pathway explains its prerequisites and qualification process.

The journey should be enjoyable even if the visitor never pursues that profession. It should not lock a character into a class, assign a permanent identity, or make a military branch the default next step.

## Design and implementation boundaries

- This is a candidate model, not a settled canon or implemented credential system.
- Experiences defines the meaning of learning, pathways, and qualifications in this world.
- Any authoritative credential, assessment, identity, or authorization contract must be defined in the appropriate domain rather than simulated as UI state.
- Presentation may show progress and explain standards, but must not invent qualifications or authority.
- Physics, GUI, FSM_COS, and host applications retain their existing architectural ownership; this document does not create parallel frameworks.

## Open questions

- Which disciplines and foundational projects belong in the first demonstrable learning slice?
- What minimum evidence should be retained for a qualification, and who is trusted to issue it?
- How should credentials transfer between jurisdictions and institutions?
- Which skills require periodic recertification or demonstrated recent practice?
- How do privacy, consent, safeguarding, and age-appropriate experiences constrain learner records and social systems?
- Which learning outcomes should persist across sessions, and which can remain local to a particular activity?

The intended outcome is a galaxy where people can discover what interests them, become genuinely capable, and choose how they contribute—without a single prescribed destiny.
