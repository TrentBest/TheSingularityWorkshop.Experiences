# Mission Loop and Consequence Model

**Status:** candidate design contract.

## The rhythm

The signature rhythm is:

**Survive → Discover → Solve → Prepare → Execute → Change the Galaxy**

It describes a connected Experience arc. It is not a requirement that every mission be linear or that every phase be a separate level.

### 1. Survive

The visitor enters a situation with limited resources, incomplete information, and immediate constraints. Survival establishes stakes and gives the visitor a reason to pay attention.

### 2. Discover

The apparent mission is not necessarily the real mission. The visitor learns more about the location, people, adversary, environment, and larger situation. New evidence may change the objective.

### 3. Solve

The visitor determines what must change and how. Solutions may involve engineering, negotiation, investigation, rescue, logistics, tactical action, or combinations of these.

### 4. Prepare

Preparation is meaningful work: assemble a team, select equipment, repair or modify systems, acquire intelligence, plan routes, secure resources, and establish contingencies.

### 5. Execute

Execution should be embodied and consequential. The visitor's position, timing, coordination, momentum, equipment configuration, and decisions should matter. The simulation must resolve outcomes from declared rules rather than scripted spectacle presented as physics.

### 6. Change the Galaxy

The result becomes part of persistent world state. A mission may affect:
- knowledge and evidence;
- relationships, trust, and reputation;
- access to places, services, or institutions;
- equipment, resources, and supply;
- local safety, ownership, or political control;
- faction relationships, alliances, and strategic options.

Not every outcome needs to affect every category. The important rule is that consequences are explicit, attributable, and discoverable.

## Mission truth

A mission record should distinguish:
- the initial briefing and who authored it;
- what the visitor actually knew at each decision point;
- evidence discovered during the mission;
- the objective as it changed;
- decisions and actions taken;
- the rules and simulation version used to resolve them;
- the resulting world-state changes.

The briefing is not omniscient truth. It is a claim from a source with a perspective and possibly incomplete information.

## Branching and replay

A visitor may revisit a mission as an observer, inspect alternate plans, or compare recorded outcomes. A replay or hypothetical must not silently rewrite the authoritative world. Branches and simulations should be clearly identified as alternate runs unless explicitly committed.

## Open questions

- Which mission consequences are global, regional, factional, or personal?
- How are delayed consequences represented?
- Can missions be solved without combat, and what does the simulation require to validate those solutions?
- How are failure, retreat, capture, rescue, and partial success represented?
- What evidence is retained to explain why the galaxy changed?
