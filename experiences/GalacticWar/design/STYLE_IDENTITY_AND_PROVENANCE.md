# Style, Identity, and Provenance

**Status:** candidate design direction. Exact ownership and transfer rules require a separate authoritative contract.

## The visual promise

The universe should celebrate unapologetic individuality. Its heroes and villains have the kind of presence normally reserved for legends: exaggerated silhouettes, dramatic proportions, signature weapons, memorable fashion, deliberate posture, and details that remain recognizable at a distance.

A character might carry a weapon whose size seems ridiculous by ordinary standards, wear a striking coat or hat, or combine materials and ornament in a way no one else would dare. The design should make these choices feel intentional and physically present, not like interchangeable loot layered onto a generic avatar.

The live-action adaptation of *One Piece* is a useful reference for translating extravagant anime character design into tangible people, costumes, props, and environments. This is a reference for spirit and execution, not a request to reproduce protected characters, costumes, symbols, or designs. The aim is original characters with equally strong visual conviction.

## Identity is a composition

A person's recognizable identity can be expressed through:
- body language, stance, movement, and presence;
- clothing, armor, materials, silhouette, and personal grooming;
- signature tools, weapons, and equipment;
- flags, colors, insignia, heraldry, and faction marks;
- decorations, trophies, keepsakes, and objects with known histories;
- the visual language of their ship, crew, and home.

A faction may establish standards, but individuals should be able to interpret, embellish, resist, or visibly reject them. Distinctiveness should not be reduced to a rarity tier or a randomized color palette.

## Scarcity with provenance

Some decorations and objects should be truly finite. A trophy may have a fixed number of legitimate instances in the entire persistent universe—for example, exactly 200. The system must know which instances exist, who currently possesses each one, and how each changed hands.

For a designated PvP trophy, the intended fantasy is direct and consequential: the only way to obtain an existing instance is to defeat its current holder (or another eligible holder) under the declared world rules and take possession of that instance. There is no vendor copy, unlimited crafting recipe, cash-shop duplicate, or hidden replenishment that makes the claim meaningless.

This creates a visible lineage:
- a defined original issuance or creation event;
- a fixed instance identity and immutable provenance record;
- a current owner and a history of previous owners;
- a transfer event with a cause and evidence;
- clear rules for what happens on defeat, capture, account absence, deletion, or other exceptional cases.

The decoration is not simply “rare.” It is a story that can be read from the object.

## Design principles for finite trophies

1. **Finite means finite.** The authoritative world must enforce the cap across all servers or shards that claim to share the same universe.
2. **Instances are identifiable.** Each trophy instance has its own stable identity, not merely a shared item definition.
3. **Ownership is authoritative.** Client visuals or inventory edits cannot create, duplicate, or transfer a trophy.
4. **Transfer is earned in-world.** For trophy classes explicitly designated as combat-takeable, the qualifying defeat and transfer must be validated by authoritative combat and ownership rules.
5. **Provenance survives transfer.** The previous owner's history is not erased when the object changes hands.
6. **No quiet duplication.** Restoration, rollback, support intervention, and server recovery must preserve the finite-instance invariant and produce auditable records.
7. **Rules are disclosed.** Players should know which items can be taken, under what conditions, and what defeat means before risking them.

The exact defeat conditions, treatment of disconnects, safe zones, team assists, repeated farming, collusion, account compromise, and protection against coercive abuse are unresolved design contracts. They must be solved without quietly diluting the core promise of genuine scarcity and earned possession.

## Ships have identity too

Ships should carry their owner's and crew's identity through silhouette, engineering choices, paint, banners, markings, trophies, wear, repairs, and visible history. A ship encountered in space should be recognizable before its nameplate is read. Captured or inherited decorations can make its appearance a record of past encounters.

## Questions requiring illumination

- Which object categories are finite trophies, and which are freely reproducible personal expression?
- Is the cap global to the whole galaxy, or scoped to a clearly defined persistent universe?
- Does a qualifying defeat transfer the actual object, or create a claim/recovery process?
- What happens when an owner is offline, inactive, disconnected, or permanently leaves?
- Can trophies be displayed, hidden, mounted on ships, or passed through inheritance?
- How are combat-takeable objects protected from exploits without removing the meaningful risk?
- How do factions, cultures, and individual styles remain visually distinct without reducing identity to faction uniforms?
