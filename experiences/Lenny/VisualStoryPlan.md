# Little Lenny — Character and Visual Story Plan

**Status:** Working visual-development brief derived from the manuscript. This is not approved final character artwork.

## Purpose

Develop a coherent visual language for all 33 story pages without waiting for the original PDF and without silently inventing biographical details the manuscript does not provide. The manuscript remains authoritative for events, relationships, emotional turns, and sequence. This brief translates those facts into repeatable visual direction.

## Canon, interpretation, and open design

Use three levels of certainty:

- **Manuscript canon:** explicitly stated or directly observable in the story.
- **Visual interpretation:** a staging, expression, pose, or composition chosen to communicate a canonical moment. It may be revised without changing the story.
- **Open character design:** physical traits or costume details the manuscript does not establish. Keep these neutral and consistent in early concept work; do not present a guess as author-approved canon.

### Lenny

**Manuscript canon**
- A small, young boy.
- He plays in warm beach sand, digs holes, fills them with ocean water, and carries a bucket and shovel.
- He is energetic and curious; he runs to investigate what his dad has found.
- He becomes eager to help, then frustrated and discouraged when the beach becomes littered again and people do not respond.
- He is compassionate toward sea life, persistent, expressive, and ultimately hopeful.
- He carries or uses a small trash bag during the cleanup and ocean adventure.

**Visual performance**
- Make his small stature legible beside his father and the beach crowd.
- Use clear, readable body language: forward-leaning curiosity; quick, purposeful movement; open gestures when recruiting help; lowered gaze and folded posture when discouraged; renewed lift and relaxed movement at the end.
- Keep the bucket, shovel, and small cleanup bag distinct props. The bag should remain visibly small beside the scale of the ocean problem.
- His emotional arc is the visual anchor: curiosity → concern → enthusiasm → overwhelm → wonder → empathy → hopeful resolve.

**Open design decisions**
- Exact age, height, face shape, skin tone, hair, eye color, clothing, footwear, and any signature accessories are not specified. Do not lock these details as canon without author input.
- Establish one repeatable model sheet once an author-approved design direction exists. Until then, use a deliberately simple, non-specific concept silhouette and record any temporary choices as provisional.

### Lenny's dad

**Manuscript canon**
- Lenny's father accompanies him to the beach and is actively involved in the cleanup.
- He picks up litter, carries the trash bag, explains the harm caused by pollution, encourages Lenny, kneels beside him, places an arm around him, catches him before a wave, and carries him on his shoulders.
- His manner is patient, reassuring, protective, and capable of laughing with Lenny.

**Visual performance**
- Clearly taller and physically larger than Lenny, with a calm, reassuring presence.
- Repeat recognizable gestures: bending or kneeling to meet Lenny at eye level; a supportive arm or hand on the shoulder; protective reach near the water; carrying Lenny securely on his shoulders.
- Keep his design visually stable across beach scenes and the underwater journey.

**Open design decisions**
- Exact age, build, face, hair, skin tone, clothing, footwear, and accessories are not specified. Do not infer a specific appearance or family resemblance as established fact.

### Dolphin and pod

**Manuscript canon**
- A dolphin first appears bobbing its head above the waves and seems to smile at Lenny.
- It retrieves litter and tosses it into Lenny's bag.
- A pod accompanies Lenny and his dad; dolphins create a bubble of air, communicate telepathically, guide them through damaged marine environments, and return them safely to the beach.
- The dolphin later balances litter on its nose, tosses it into the bag, waves a fin, and dives away.

**Visual performance**
- Give the first-seen dolphin a clear, memorable silhouette and friendly, expressive behavior without anthropomorphizing it into a human character.
- Differentiate the pod through position, scale, and movement rather than arbitrary costume or markings.
- Make the protective bubble visually understandable and gentle; it should read as a safe space around Lenny and his dad.
- The dolphin's repeated nose-toss gesture can become a recognizable visual motif.

**Open design decisions**
- Species-specific markings, exact coloration, individual identity marks, and pod size are not specified. Use naturalistic dolphin anatomy and keep any distinguishing marks provisional.

## Visual continuity rules

1. Keep character proportions, face construction, hair, clothing, and props consistent from page to page once approved.
2. Keep the beach geography coherent: shoreline, waterline, sand, horizon, and the family's relative position should not jump arbitrarily.
3. Track the bucket and shovel in the opening beach play; track the trash bag from the cleanup onward. Do not teleport or multiply props.
4. The trash bag begins as a practical small bag held by Dad; Lenny later carries it. Its scale helps communicate the difference between one child's effort and the size of the problem.
5. The dolphins' arrival should grow naturally from the litter appearing beside Lenny and Dad. Preserve the reveal on page 21.
6. The underwater sequence should feel wondrous but emotionally honest. Show environmental harm without turning injured animals into graphic spectacle.
7. Keep the story's emotional focus on care, cooperation, and achievable action. Do not make the beachgoers caricature villains.
8. No image should introduce a new plot event, character relationship, species, location, or object that changes the manuscript.
9. Compose illustrations to support the words, not replace or rewrite them. Leave readable space for text when page layout is eventually known.
10. Distinguish confirmed facts from provisional visual choices in all reusable prompts and asset metadata.

## StoryBuilder connection

StoryBuilder's useful model is not a reason to make this reader depend on StoryBuilder at runtime. Keep the authored Experience self-contained while borrowing the authoring discipline:

- Give each character and visual asset a stable identity.
- Keep manuscript facts separate from provisional art decisions.
- Link scene descriptions to their manuscript page numbers.
- Track provenance for every accepted character-design decision.
- Preserve revisions as decisions, rather than silently rewriting established canon.

A future StoryBuilder project can hold the richer world bible and asset provenance. This document is the lightweight, repository-local visual brief for the Lenny Experience.
