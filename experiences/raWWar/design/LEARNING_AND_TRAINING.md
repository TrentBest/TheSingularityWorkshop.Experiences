# Learning, Training, and Vocation

**Design status:** candidate direction grounded in the raWWar Experience vision. This document describes intended world behavior, not an implemented curriculum or training simulator.

## The central idea

In this civilization, people begin learning the systems of their future early—not because every child is being recruited into war, but because the world around them is technologically complex and participation is a normal part of civic life.

Learning is continuous and situated. A person encounters a concept, sees it used in the world, experiments with a safe model, develops practical skill, and later chooses whether to pursue it as a vocation.

The visitor should feel that education is opening an immense world rather than pushing them down a predetermined career.

## A progression of agency

1. **Discover:** encounter materials, machines, energy, motion, communication, ecology, history, and social systems through playful exhibits.
2. **Understand:** inspect a system, ask questions, compare designs, and learn the principles behind its behavior.
3. **Practice:** use guided simulations, manipulable models, and ghost demonstrations to rehearse procedures without confusing rehearsal with real-world qualification.
4. **Specialize:** explore vocations across engineering, fabrication, medicine, science, logistics, navigation, governance, fleet operations, and military service.
5. **Qualify:** demonstrate the specific knowledge and practical competence required for a role or vehicle position.
6. **Serve and contribute:** take on responsibilities, gain experience, and build a record of actual performance.
7. **Teach and improve:** mentor others, contribute research, refine tools, or help institutions evolve.

No age threshold, curriculum duration, or mandatory military pathway is established by this concept document.

## Diegetic training

Training belongs inside the world. It should be experienced through believable institutions, equipment, instructors, and schedules—not as an out-of-world skill tree.

A training environment may use a VR or simulation layer interleaved with believable time in the world. A learner can pause or interrupt ordinary career exploration; advanced combat or specialist instruction should have clear prerequisites rather than silently granting competence.

Ghost avatars are instructors made visible: they demonstrate position, timing, rotation, coordination, and procedure. They can show how a person occupies a station, how a crew hands off control, how an assembly is serviced, or how a squad moves through a task.

A ghost demonstration is instruction, not proof that the learner can perform the task.

## Qualification, experience, and performance

These are separate facts and must remain separate in the domain model:

- **Qualification:** a verified authorization or demonstrated competency for a defined role or station.
- **Experience:** accumulated participation, practice, and exposure.
- **Performance:** evidence of how the person actually performed in a particular context.

Time served must not automatically imply qualification. Qualification must not guarantee excellent performance. A strong performance in one situation must not erase missing prerequisites in another.

For vehicle and exoskeleton operations, each crew position should have its own responsibilities and qualifications. Training should cover every position, not merely the vehicle as one undifferentiated skill.

## Learning through engineered objects

The exoskeleton is a natural learning environment because its structure connects many disciplines:

- materials and structural engineering;
- mechanics, balance, and locomotion;
- power, thermal management, and life support;
- sensors, signal processing, and control;
- ergonomics, human factors, and safety;
- manufacturing, maintenance, and repair;
- tactics and teamwork, where appropriate to the learner's role.

A visitor should be able to select a component, inspect its geometry and purpose, compare research variants, see obstructions and sensor views, and test a proposed improvement in a controlled model.

For example, a learner might inspect an armor plate, observe how heat moves through it, test a material or cooling change, and compare the resulting thermal behavior. The demonstration should reveal the model and its limitations rather than presenting an unexplained upgrade animation.

## Career choice is a living-world interaction

Career discovery should feel like visiting a jobs board, speaking with institutions, and seeing real prerequisites. The learner can inspect a role, understand what it contributes, see the required qualifications, and choose a path.

A career change should be freely interruptible at the exploration stage. Entering a role, receiving authority, or taking responsibility may require explicit qualification and acceptance. The Experience should make the difference visible.

## Open questions

- Which learning experiences are universal civic education, and which are institution-specific?
- How do age, consent, guardianship, and safety shape access to advanced simulations?
- Which qualifications are portable between factions or institutions?
- How are refresher training and qualification expiry represented?
- How are team, squad, and crew competencies represented without erasing individual accountability?
- Which training experiences are physical, simulated, mentored, or mixed?

These questions should be answered by explicit contracts and small validated prototypes, not implied by UI progression.
